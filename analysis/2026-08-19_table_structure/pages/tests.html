Yes, in the dyadic table, and depth sharpens it. 0.72 to 0.992 by depth 5, over 27 points, between two series with no common input. Triadic is far messier, dipping to 0.19 before recovering at depth 10 — exactly where test 03 puts its crossover, though only ten points remain there.

Caveat that travels with this: correlation is scale-free. It says the shape tracks, not the amplitude. O34 measured 94 percent at d0 falling to 80 at d6 — falling where this rises — and both are true, because that measure is sensitive to magnitude and this one is not.

Dyadic reaches the tightest agreement while having far less data than the sub-integer bases. Base 2 climbs to 0.99 and holds it across depths 4–7 on 25-plus points. The finest base has six times the rungs and 190 usable depths, yet plateaus at 0.95 and never gets closer. More rungs, better statistics, worse agreement — the same direction as everything else here: finer sampling dilutes the structure rather than sharpening it.

Base 3 stays the odd one, dipping to 0.19 in the middle over eleven usable depths, so that dip is real and not small-sample noise. Nothing should be read into bases 5 through 9 — four, three, two, one and one usable depth. Base 8's −0.77 and base 9's +0.84 are single correlations over ten points, not curves. At a ceiling of 232 there is not enough ladder for them to be anything more.

**Question.** With no file of zeros read, do the residuals of the table along the doublings show the same turnings that the steps of the staircase line up at?

**What ran.** `run/tablezeros.py`, through `run/run.sh`, on the repo's cache of the count at the doublings. EXPLORATORY: no prereg, no decision rule, no verdict. **What it is.** Two readings of the one staircase, with no zero taken from outside. The steps, turned, line up at certain heights. The table's residuals along the doublings turn at angles, and those angles are the folded images of those heights within what this many rows can resolve. With the lowest three or five heights the staircase's angles account for more of the table's residual than any of the random draws did. The zeros of zeta are the heights the first reading settles on as the cutoff goes to infinity; the table holds them folded onto its own ladder, in the rows it has.

**What it is not.** The reading is coarse. The gaps found are small beside the resolution, but the table's spectrum has many local maxima and the run did not count how close a random angle falls to one, so a nearby maximum is weak evidence taken alone; the share accounted for is the stronger of the two readings, and with the lowest peak alone it is only at the edge of what random angles reach. The table's amplitudes at those angles do not follow the weights the heights would predict, `tablezeros.peaks[0].table_amplitude_there` 0.0922 against `tablezeros.peaks[0].weight_2_over_height` 0.1414 at the lowest. The level removed was `tablezeros.level` -0.8018. No test of the Riemann hypothesis is made.

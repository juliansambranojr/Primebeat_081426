Section 04's analyticity argument is wrong in its strong form. It states the smoothness statistic is "not measuring the data" and returns the same value regardless. A 91-centre placebo sweep (lab_notebook entry 6, instrument O13_smoothness_null.py) shows max_z does vary, range 1.30. Primebeat · O9 convergence abscissa

What the O9 control actually measures

Three tests run against the convergence-abscissa experiment. The regression-variable explanation is falsified, the control turns out to be ill-posed, and part 3's null is structural rather than measured.

2026-08-15 O9_convergence_abscissa.py c=13 · pmax=100000 · 9592 primes

SUMSummary

Six bug fixes were applied to O9 and the script was rerun. Three follow-up tests were then run to check whether the part-2 control failure was explicable and whether part 3's negative result on σ = 1/2 is trustworthy. Part 1 is unaffected throughout. The headline findings, in the order they were established:

  • The log log N explanation is falsified. Switching the regression variable from log N to log pN moves the zero crossing by 5.3×10−5. It cannot move it, for a structural reason given below.
  • The control is ill-posed, not merely off-target. The crossing is not a single number: it ranges from 1.177 at t = 14.5 to 0.491 at t = 80. The aggregate 0.8814 is an artifact of averaging four incommensurable columns, two of which are noise.
  • Part 3's null is guaranteed by construction. For a fixed finite prime set the fitted slope is real-analytic in σ, so a kink at σ = 0.500 is impossible at finite truncation. The smoothness test returns max_z ≈ 2.5 on clean data and on pure noise alike.

Consequence for the write-up

The conclusion — that the Beat's optimum at 1/2 comes from the functional equation's reflection axis rather than from convergence — may well be true. Part 3 is not evidence for it. Claiming a measured negative would be overclaiming.

01Test — the regression variable falsified

Hypothesis under test. decay_exponent regresses log|S2N − SN| against log N, where N is the prime count. The summed terms are p−σ and pN ~ N ln N, so the proposed correct variable is log pN = log N + log log N, with the missing term contaminating the slope and displacing the crossing from 1.000 to 0.883.

Pre-registered before running: (A) crossing moves to 1.00 ± 0.02 → contamination was the whole story; (B) moves to ~0.93 → coefficient explained, intercept not; (C) does not move → explanation wrong.

A positive rescaling cannot move a zero. Equivalently, the crossing is the ratio of the low-σ fit's intercept to its coefficient, and both are scaled by the same factor:

count:  0.9441 / 1.0752 = 0.878
prime:  0.8177 / 0.9313 = 0.878

The offset survives exactly. It is a property of the data, not of the axis, and no choice of regression variable will fix it. The prediction bands were mis-specified — (A) was structurally unreachable, and that should have been caught before the test was framed.

Residual puzzle. Under the corrected variable theory predicts a coefficient of exactly −1.000. It comes out −0.931. The simple |Δ| ~ N^a · p_N^(−σ) model does not close even after fixing the axis, and there is currently no account of the remainder.

02Test — t-dependence of the crossing control ill-posed

With the axis ruled out, the remaining candidate was partial phase cancellation: the σ = 0 exponent sitting near 0.944 rather than 1.000 because the block sum only partially cancels. That predicts a t-dependence — larger t means faster winding of t log p across a dyadic block, so more decoherence and a lower crossing.

The aggregate crossing is an averaging artifact

The spread is 0.685 in σ — not a correction, a collapse. The reported 0.8814 is the crossing of the mean slope column; the mean of the four per-t crossings is 0.8967, a different number, because averaging slopes and then finding a zero is not the same operation as finding zeros and averaging.

Further, the two well-fitted columns cross above 1.0, at 1.177 and 1.126. Every proposed explanation — log log N contamination, phase cancellation — predicts a crossing below 1.0. Only the high-t columns drag the mean down.

The statistic is not measuring the data

All sixteen cases land between 2.36 and 2.62, across columns whose residual scales differ by three orders of magnitude — 3.99e−09 at t = 30 against 1.86e−06 at t = 80. The t = 80 column, whose underlying regression has r² = 0.0004, returns max_z = 2.5620 — indistinguishable from the cleanest column.

A statistic that returns ≈2.5 on clean data and ≈2.5 on pure noise is measuring the geometry of its own fit, not the phenomenon.

The reason is analytic, not empirical

For a fixed finite set of primes, Σ p−σ−it is entire in σ. So |S2N − SN| is real-analytic in σ wherever it is nonzero, its log is real-analytic, and the least-squares slope — a fixed linear combination of those logs — is real-analytic too. A kink at σ = 0.500 is not merely absent from the data; it is impossible at finite truncation. The fine sweep is a smooth analytic curve by construction and the cubic absorbs it. The residual ≈2.5 is cubic truncation error over the window, which is exactly why it is the same number regardless of what lies underneath.

The in-window values confirm it. First differences of the t = 14.5 slope column across σ = 0.48 … 0.52:

A straight line to six digits against values of ~0.72, with nothing happening at 0.500.

So part 3 cannot distinguish "σ = 1/2 is not distinguished in the divergence" from "this design cannot see structure anywhere." The null was fixed before any prime was summed. The xvar invariance (2.6080 → 2.6082) and the per-t null are both real, but they are invariance under changes that could not have mattered.

05Status of each claim

unaffected

Part 1 — partial-sum stability vs σ

Untouched by any of the above. The relative |S2N − SN|/|SN| measurement does not use the log-log regression.

ill-posed

Part 2 — the decay-exponent control

Neither passed nor failed. It is a mean over four curves whose individual crossings span 0.685 in σ, two of which are fitted at r² < 0.5. "The control failed at 0.88" and "the control passed" are both wrong statements about it.

not evidence

Part 3 — σ = 1/2 not distinguished

The verdict is correct as a statement about the computed curve and useless as a test of the hypothesis. Any finite-truncation σ-sweep returns "smooth" here for analytic reasons.

open

The Beat's optimum at 1/2

May still originate in the functional equation's reflection axis rather than in convergence. That claim now has no supporting measurement from O9.

06What would actually test it

Structure at σ = 1/2, if it exists, lives in the truncation limit — which is precisely where the Euler product stops being a sum. A test has to involve N → ∞ behaviour rather than a σ-sweep at fixed N: how the fit degrades along the ladder as N grows, and whether the rate of that degradation carries a feature at 1/2. That is what part 2 was reaching for before its control turned out to be ill-posed.

Two prerequisites before that is worth building:

  • Fix the aggregation. Weight or drop columns by fit quality rather than taking an unweighted mean over t. As it stands t = 80 contributes equally to every reported number while explaining 0.04% of its own variance.
  • Extend the ladder. Six rungs capping at N = 4000 cannot resolve a power law at the larger t values. The ladder, not --pmax, is the binding constraint — raising --pmax above ~68000 currently buys nothing.

07Corrections made in the course of this audit

Recorded because each was a wrong intermediate claim that a later measurement overturned.

estimate

The pre-fix max_z was estimated at ≈3.2 by inverting the ddof factor alone, implying the verdict had flipped. Exact recomputation gave 1.997 — the window fix also moved max_departure, and the two fixes push in opposite directions. The verdict never flipped.

prediction

Outcome band (A) — crossing moves to 1.00 — was structurally unreachable, since the reparametrization is a constant positive rescaling. The test could only ever return (C).

assessment

Part 3 was described as having "got stronger" on the strength of its xvar invariance and per-t null. Both hold, but neither is informative — see section 04.

**Question.** The second lead the handoff names: an exact identity tying the prime counts at powers of two, modulo small primes, to the harmonic structure. Unit 0617 (exact-certificates) found Lucas congruences on the cells only at the rate of chance. Three steps further: whether the residues of the prime counts themselves carry the harmonics, how precise the zeros' formula must be to fix a residue, and an exact identity for the residue modulo two.

**What ran.** `run/resid.py`, by `run/run.sh`, through `lab run`, on the exact prime counts at powers of two to row ninety-two and Odlyzko's first hundred thousand zeros. The residues of the counts and of the rows modulo two, three, five and seven were tested against four harmonic predictors at each row (the sign of the whole zero sum, the sign of the near-harmonic part, the parity of the row, the leader's phase in bins): a chi-square per pair, its p-value from twenty thousand row permutations. The precision test is Riemann's explicit formula with R at each zero cut to its first five Moebius terms. The identity: the number of squarefree divisors of n is one at n = 1, two at a prime power and a multiple of four otherwise, so the sum of that count up to x is one plus twice the number of prime powers up to x, modulo four; and that sum is a Moebius-weighted sum of divisor-function sums, each a count of lattice points under a hyperbola.

**The residues do not see the harmonics.** Of `resid.residue_tests` 32 tests none falls below a p-value of five percent (`resid.residue_pvalue_below_0.05` 0); the smallest is `resid.residue_pvalue_min` 0.0626, the count modulo seven against the parity of the row. The counts are even `resid.pi_mod2_counts[0]` 51 times and odd `resid.pi_mod2_counts[1]` 41 times; modulo three they fall `resid.pi_mod3_counts[0]` 27, `resid.pi_mod3_counts[1]` 29 and `resid.pi_mod3_counts[2]` 36 times in the three classes.

**The zeros' formula fixes a residue only where it is exact, and it stops being exact early.** With all `resid.precision.rows41_92` 52 rows from forty-one on as the test, the formula with the first hundred thousand zeros rounds to the exact count through row `resid.precision.K100000.exact_from_row2_through` 20, with ten thousand through row `resid.precision.K10000.exact_from_row2_through` 15, with two thousand through row `resid.precision.K2000.exact_from_row2_through` 14. Its error grows with the row at `resid.precision.K100000.log2_err_slope_rows30_92` 0.474 in the base-two logarithm per row with a hundred thousand zeros (`resid.precision.K2000.log2_err_slope_rows30_92` 0.494 with two thousand): close to half a power of two per row, the square root of the count. At row ninety-two it is `resid.precision.K100000.abs_err_row92` 6340000000.0. The parity it gives is right on `resid.precision.K100000.parity_right_rows41_92` 26 of the fifty-two rows, `resid.precision.K10000.parity_right_rows41_92` 24 with ten thousand zeros and `resid.precision.K2000.parity_right_rows41_92` 25 with two thousand: chance.

**The parity identity is exact and costs the square root.** It gives the parity of the prime count at every power of two from the second to the forty-sixth, `resid.parity_identity.rows_agree` 45 of `resid.parity_identity.rows` 45 against the list. Its time grows by `meta.resid.parity_identity.log2_time_slope_per_row` 0.484 in the base-two logarithm per row, `meta.resid.parity_identity.seconds.46` 0.544 seconds at row forty-six; carried to row ninety-three that is ten to the `meta.resid.parity_identity.extrapolated_log10_seconds_row93` 6.6 seconds in numpy on this machine, timed while another run shared it. After its main term the divisor sum leaves a remainder of median `resid.parity_identity.remainder_over_x14_rows20_46.median_abs` 0.804 times the fourth root of x over rows twenty to forty-six, at most `resid.parity_identity.remainder_over_x14_rows20_46.max_abs` 2.851; against the square root it is `resid.parity_identity.remainder_over_x12_rows20_46.median_abs` 0.0025. At row forty-six the remainder is `resid.parity_identity.remainder_row46` 4373.0, and the identity needs the sum to the unit.

**What it shows.** The residues of the prime counts at powers of two carry none of the harmonic structure the tests can see, and the zeros' formula fixes them only where it fixes the count itself, which past row twenty takes more zeros than the table holds. The exact identity that exists for the parity goes through a lattice count. The Dirichlet series of the squarefree-divisor count is the square of zeta over zeta at twice the argument, so the zeros enter it only at half their height and at the fourth root of x, a remainder the measurements put at that size, while the identity needs that sum to the unit. It is tied to the divisor problem and has no link to the odd harmonics of the depth comb. What it does give is exact: the parity of each row past ninety-two, one row at a time at the cost of the square root, and with it, by unit 0617's parity certificate, the odd corner cells those rows complete. That reaches finitely many rows; the corner past them stays open. Exploratory; no outcome marked.

**Question.** Unit 0642 (corner-edge-excess) found about a third more cells near zero on the corner's edges than its benchmark, which gave each cell its own independent phases. The Mac session's box asked whether the zeros' known correlation explains that excess; working it showed a prior question, which Julian chose (corner question 8, answer (a)): is the excess real at all, measured against the spread one table really has?

**What ran.** Three studies and one locked test, their outputs copied into `run/` as produced and checked against the tree by `run/check.py` (`copies.all_match` true). The power study of the correlation box (`O101_edge_correlation_power.py`, synthetic heights only); the power study and a design comparison for the test (`O102_edge_excess_frozen_phase_power.py`, `analysis/2026-11-28/o102_designs/`, synthetic heights only); and the test itself, `O102_edge_excess_frozen_phase.py` under `preregs/edge_excess_frozen_phase_v1_20261128.md`, locked on Julian's word before it ran. Its null keeps the true first hundred thousand zeros, their sizes and each zero's pattern across the cells, and turns each zero by one random phase shared by every cell it touches: the correlation between cells that the benchmark of unit 0642 (corner-edge-excess) left out.

**The correlation box could not be decided.** With a GUE set of heights as the truth, the drafted rule said "explained" `O101_edge_correlation_power.summary.rule_rates.truth GUE.explained` 0.0 of the time and "ambiguous" `O101_edge_correlation_power.summary.rule_rates.truth GUE.ambiguous` 0.9: one set of heights gives near-zero counts that swing several-fold with its lowest heights, so no single table can tell GUE from Poisson by this count.

**One table's count spreads by about four tenths of itself.** Against shared random phases a synthetic table's near-zero count has a spread of `O102_edge_excess_frozen_phase_power.summary.null_sd_over_mean_median` 0.382 of its mean; the rule then fires "excess real" on a count raised a third `O102_edge_excess_frozen_phase_power.summary.rule_rates.raised a third.excess_real` 0.0 of the time and on one raised two thirds `O102_edge_excess_frozen_phase_power.summary.rule_rates.raised two thirds.excess_real` 0.2. Pooling the thresholds and all fourteen diagonals leaves the spread at `O102_designs.D1 pooled thresholds, all diagonals.sd_over_mean_median` 0.379; holding the first ten zeros' phases at `O102_designs.D2 first 10 zeros fixed.sd_over_mean_median` 0.429, the first fifty at `O102_designs.D3 first 50 zeros fixed.sd_over_mean_median` 0.416. The near-zero cells come in runs, and the edges share them, so a table holds few independent near-zero events.

**The test: the observed excess lies inside that spread.** The observed count over the twelve edges is `O102_edge_excess_frozen_phase.summary.primary.observed` 313, equal to the count of the earlier unit, as the run's integrity check confirms. Over a thousand shared-phase draws the count has mean `O102_edge_excess_frozen_phase.summary.primary.null.mean` 239.87 and standard deviation `O102_edge_excess_frozen_phase.summary.primary.null.sd` 97.35, from `O102_edge_excess_frozen_phase.summary.primary.null.q05` 117.0 to `O102_edge_excess_frozen_phase.summary.primary.null.q95` 429.0 between the fifth and ninety-fifth percentiles; p is `O102_edge_excess_frozen_phase.summary.primary.p` 0.1878121878121878. The mean is the earlier benchmark's again; the spread is several times what that benchmark allowed. At a fiftieth, observed `O102_edge_excess_frozen_phase.summary.thresholds.0.02.observed` 123 against mean `O102_edge_excess_frozen_phase.summary.thresholds.0.02.null.mean` 94.85, p `O102_edge_excess_frozen_phase.summary.thresholds.0.02.p` 0.18681318681318682; at a tenth, `O102_edge_excess_frozen_phase.summary.thresholds.0.1.observed` 616 against `O102_edge_excess_frozen_phase.summary.thresholds.0.1.null.mean` 481.51, p `O102_edge_excess_frozen_phase.summary.thresholds.0.1.p` 0.19480519480519481. Edge by edge it is the same: at depth plus one `O102_edge_excess_frozen_phase.summary.per_diagonal_005.edge1.observed` 28 cells, p `O102_edge_excess_frozen_phase.summary.per_diagonal_005.edge1.p` 0.15584415584415584. The decision rule's output is `O102_edge_excess_frozen_phase.summary.decision_rule_output` "consistent_with_chance"; the verdict line in the prereg is Julian's.

**What it shows.** The edge excess of unit 0642 is what one table of these zeros gives by the luck of their phases. Its benchmark had the right mean and far too narrow a spread, so its statement that the excess is real in count does not stand. The test could not have confirmed an excess of a third, so it says the excess is unproven, not that it is absent; a test that could see it needs independent replicates, which the multi-base design check is measuring. Exploratory, apart from the locked test, whose verdict is Julian's.
